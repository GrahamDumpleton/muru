`muructl debug status` shows what each side of a worker holds as of the moment it answers: THREADS and THREAD-OLDEST for the application threads, counting a request held at `breakpoint()` or streaming its response, and LOOP and LOOP-OLDEST for the event loop, open WebSockets left out, in place of one IN-FLIGHT count and an OLDEST age that could be a second old and left exempt requests out. A worker that does not answer within a second is shown from its last report, marked with a star. STATE is `starting`, `ready`, `debugging`, `replacing` or `retiring`, ROUTED says whether the edge sends it requests, and `--json` carries `state`, `routed`, `threads`, `loop` and `stale` in place of `replaced`, `in_flight` and `oldest_ms`. The dashboard's workers page shows the same. Every header of a `muructl` table is one word now: `QUEUE-P95` in `scale status` and `REQUEST-ID` in `websockets --list`.
